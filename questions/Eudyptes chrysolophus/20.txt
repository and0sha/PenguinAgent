The observation of deep burrowing would represent a profound anomaly for this strictly surface-nesting species.

The Macaroni penguin exhibits extreme egg dimorphism, and it is a nearly ubiquitous life-history trait for the A-egg to be lost, ejected, or suffer disproportionate mortality before hatching.

3. Are two chicks being reared simultaneously and provisioned equally within a single nest? This species exhibits obligate brood reduction where a maximum of one chick is successfully reared per nest.

4. Is the adult male provisioning the newly hatched chick during the first 23 to 25 days of its life? Such behavior is a stark deviation from their normal breeding biology, where the male exclusively broods and guards the chick while enduring a prolonged fast, leaving the female to perform all foraging trips.

5. Is the foraging penguin executing extended, flat-bottomed U-dives with prolonged time at maximum depth to capture prey? Continuous telemetry indicates that Macaroni penguins capture prey almost exclusively during V-shaped "bounce" dives, executing continuous descents and ascents without pausing at the bottom.

6. Are the birds executing deep foraging dives exceeding 30 meters during the night? Their well-documented diel diving pattern restricts nocturnal dives to shallow depths of only 3 to 6 meters, making nighttime deep diving highly anomalous.

7. Is the pair engaging in copulation after the clutch has been fully laid? *Eudyptes chrysolophus* strictly confines successful copulatory behavior to the pre-laying period, making post-laying coition highly irregular for breeding individuals.

8. Does the male's ecstatic display lack the species-specific "vertical head swinging" movement? The normative courtship display requires the bird to bow low to the ground and then sweep its head and neck upward, rolling it from side to side in wide arcs while trumpeting loudly.

9. Does the adult's display call lack the complex, highly structured sequence of short syllables framed by long "marker" syllables at the beginning and end? This unique acoustic architecture is critical for individual recognition in Macaroni penguins, and its absence would indicate a severe behavioral or acoustic anomaly.

10. Is the colony exhibiting high levels of vocalization and frequent display calls during the incubation phase? Macaroni penguin colonies are typically quiet during incubation because only one parent attends the nest at a time, minimizing the need for loud mutual displays until change-overs or chick-rearing.

11. Do the prominent golden-yellow crest plumes fail to meet across the bird's forehead? Such an observation would strongly suggest hybridization or misidentification, since Macaroni penguins uniquely possess plumes that join seamlessly at the crown, unlike other crested penguins (except the Royal penguin).

12. Are adult Macaroni penguins remaining sedentary or foraging exclusively in nearshore waters during the winter months? This would be a highly anomalous event given their highly dispersive, pelagic winter migrations that are known to cover millions of square kilometers throughout the southern latitudes.

13. Are the birds exhibiting identical foraging depths and dietary proportions to sympatric Southern Rockhopper penguins (*Eudyptes chrysocome*)? This would defy their established niche segregation, where Macaroni penguins dive deeper and consume larger prey or a higher proportion of fish to avoid direct competition.

14. Is the foraging flock feeding primarily on benthic, demersal prey at the seafloor? This contradicts their obligate evolutionary specialization on pelagic, upper water-column swarming crustaceans, such as Antarctic krill (*Euphausia superba*), and mid-water myctophid fish.

15. Have the females arrived at the breeding colony prior to the males at the onset of the breeding season? The natural breeding phenology dictates that males arrive first, typically from mid-October to early November, to establish and defend the nesting territory.

16. Are parents provisioning their chicks in the crèche multiple times within a single day? This represents an abnormally high feeding frequency compared to the standard species rate of provisioning the chick every 1 to 2 days during the later crèche stage.

17. Is a pair that successfully reared and fledged a chick in the previous season exhibiting mate-switching or divorce? Macaroni penguins demonstrate a high interannual pair-bond fidelity of 71 to 79 percent, making divorce following reproductive success statistically unusual.

18. Is a male confidently identified as being under five years old successfully defending a territory and breeding? Physiological and behavioral maturity in male Macaroni penguins dictates a delayed onset of breeding, which typically commences between 5 and 6 years of age.

19. Are the birds utilizing highly organized cooperative corralling and spiraling trajectories (PO-dives) to capture schooling prey? Sophisticated three-dimensional corralling is a hallmark of *Spheniscus* penguins, whereas the pelagic, krill-hunting Macaroni penguin relies on different pursuit strategies.

20. Are the adults initiating their catastrophic post-nuptial moult at sea or on pack ice? This is contrary to their established life history, wherein they invariably return to their terrestrial breeding sites to undergo their post-nuptial moult following a brief pre-moult foraging trip.
