1. Is the observed Royal Penguin (*Eudyptes schlegeli*) found breeding or actively foraging in regions entirely outside of Macquarie Island and its adjacent sub-Antarctic waters, which is currently its sole known endemic breeding location? 

2. Does the facial plumage of the adult penguin display black or dark grey cheeks and throat, deviating from the pale grey or pure white face that clearly distinguishes it from closely related crested penguins? 

3. Are the penguin's yellow-gold superciliary crests separated across the forehead rather than meeting near the gape, an anomaly that would contradict a primary distinguishing feature separating it from all other *Eudyptes* species except the Macaroni penguin? 

4. Is the penguin failing to, or physically unable to, voluntarily raise and lower its bristly crest while dry, which is a highly unique capability documented specifically for this species? 

5. Does the underside of the penguin's flippers lack the exceptionally large amount of black coloration that is typically more extensive in *Eudyptes schlegeli* than in any other crested penguin? 

6. Is the bird successfully rearing two chicks to the fledging stage, a situation that represents a massive anomaly since crested penguins almost invariably lose the smaller first-laid A-egg or its resulting chick due to the severe energetic demands of offshore pelagic foraging? 

7. During oviposition, is the first egg (A-egg) observed to be equal to or larger than the second egg (B-egg), which would violate the extreme egg-size dimorphism inherent and deeply evolved within the *Eudyptes* genus? 

8. Is the adult provisioning its offspring with benthic prey rather than its typical, documented diet consisting almost exclusively of pelagic euphausiids (krill) and myctophids (lanternfish)? 

9. Is the individual demonstrating foraging behaviors characteristic of demersal or coastal bottom-feeding rather than its established ecological niche of subpolar, continental-insular slope and pelagic upper water column foraging? 

10. Is the bird observed roosting or remaining close to continental landmasses during the non-breeding winter period, contradicting the fact that Royal Penguins are highly pelagic outside of the breeding season and their winter whereabouts remain virtually unknown to science? 

11. During agonistic encounters, does the bird fail to use the characteristic "jab-hiss", "forward gape", or "forward trumpet" threat postures commonly utilized by this species for territorial defense? 

12. If engaged in direct physical combat, is the penguin utilizing fighting methods other than the expected "bill-jousting", pecking, and rapid striking of the opponent with its flippers? 

13. During courtship and pair-formation, is the male failing to utilize the "flag display"—a vertical head swinging motion accompanied by trumpeting—to advertise territory ownership? 

14. Are the vocalizations lacking the characteristic loud, harsh, and low-pitched acoustic structure that typifies the *Eudyptes* genus repertoire? 

15. Is the penguin observed nesting on flat, open sea-ice or sandy plains instead of its typical habitat, which involves scaling steep, rugged terrain to access dense, noisy inland colonies? 

16. In mixed or boundary zones, is the individual observed actively interbreeding or forming mixed-species pairs with Macaroni Penguins (*Eudyptes chrysolophus*), a rare event that is critical for understanding their debated taxonomic status as a separate sibling species versus a subspecies? 

17. Are incubating birds exhibiting severe physiological or behavioral stress responses, such as nest abandonment, when humans approach at distances that have been previously tested and established as safe minimum approach distance guidelines specifically for *Eudyptes schlegeli*? 

18. Does the adult's bill lack the long, slender profile and orange-brown coloration, or is the distinctive bare pink skin at the base of the bill and gape completely absent? 

19. During territory establishment, is a bird stretching its head and neck towards an intruder without performing the typical staccato cry, hiss, or up-and-down head bobbing with raised flippers in preparation for a strike? 

20. Are significant numbers of these penguins exhibiting mass mortality or signs of a novel disease epidemic, which would be a catastrophic and highly anomalous event given their highly restricted breeding range makes them extremely vulnerable to localized natural or human-induced disasters?
